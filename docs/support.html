Requirements

SaddleTrace needs an iPhone with a LiDAR scanner

That means iPhone 12 Pro or later, in the Pro or Pro Max models. The LiDAR scanner is what actually measures the animal's back — without it, the app cannot record a scan. iPhone models without LiDAR can still open, view and share scans that were imported from someone else.

SaddleTrace also requires a current version of iOS. If the App Store will not let you install it, check Settings ▸ General ▸ Software Update first.

Taking a good scan

SaddleTrace measures with a single shot rather than a sweep, so the moment you press the shutter is the whole measurement. A few things make that moment count:

  • Get the distance right. The on-screen gauge turns green when you are in range. Too close and the frame will not cover enough of the back; too far and the measurement coarsens.
  • Fill the framing box with the saddle area. The dashed box shows what will be captured. Aim for the region a saddle would actually sit on.
  • Hold the phone steady and square to the back. Holding it lower and angling upward is fine — the measurement is three-dimensional and does not need to be perfectly overhead.
  • Stand the animal square on level ground. A horse resting a hind leg will drop that hip and tilt its back, and the scan will faithfully record the tilt.
  • Good light helps the colour, not the measurement. The shape comes from the LiDAR scanner, so it works in dull light — but the photograph painted onto the model will look better in daylight.
  • Keep yourself and other people out of frame. Anything within range may be captured along with the animal.

Common questions

The app says "LiDAR Required"

Your iPhone does not have a LiDAR scanner. See the requirements above — scanning needs a Pro or Pro Max model, iPhone 12 Pro or later.

My scan includes grass, the ground, or my arm

Anything close enough to the phone can be measured along with the animal. Re-take the shot with the framing box filled by the back itself, and keep hands and lead ropes out of the frame.

Are the measurements in real units?

Yes. The LiDAR scanner measures in true metres, so distances, widths and angles are real-world values rather than a relative scale. You can switch the whole app between inches and centimetres in Settings.

Where do the section numbers start?

At the withers. Section 0 is the withers, and sections are numbered backwards from there at the spacing you set in Settings. Every distance shown in the charts and on the report is measured from the withers.

Is the printed report true scale?

The report is always drawn at an exact, stated scale, and the scale is printed on the sheet along with a scale bar. On a large page such as Tabloid or A3 that will usually be 1:1; on Letter or A4 the drawing is reduced to a clean ratio such as 1:2 so it still fits. Choose your paper size in Settings, and check the scale bar with a ruler after printing, since printer drivers sometimes shrink pages to fit the margins. Turn off any "scale to fit" or "shrink oversized pages" option in your print dialog.

What can I export?

A PDF cross-section report, DXF outlines with one layer per station, CSV files of the section outlines and of every computed measurement, an STL of the surface for CAD or 3D printing, and a colour PLY of the painted surface. Use the share buttons on a scan's result screen.

How do I send a scan to a colleague?

Open the scan and use Share Scan to produce a single archive file, then send it however you like. On their device they open SaddleTrace, tap the "..." button on the animal list, choose Import, and pick the file. They will see the same model, sections and measurements. You can also share your whole library at once from the same menu.

Will importing overwrite what I already have?

No. Importing merges. An animal you do not have is added; an animal you already have keeps its own details and gains the new scans. If an incoming scan happens to share an identifier with one you already hold, it is brought in as a separate scan rather than replacing yours.

Where are my scans stored, and are they backed up?

On your iPhone, and — if you have iCloud Drive enabled — in your own private iCloud storage, where iOS backs them up and can free up space on the phone by keeping copies in the cloud. You can see which is in use in Settings ▸ Storage inside the app. Scans are never sent to the developer.

Scans take up a lot of space

Each scan keeps its original photograph and depth data so it can be re-processed as the app improves, and that adds up. Delete scans you no longer need, or enable iCloud Drive so iOS can offload older ones from the phone while keeping them available.

Can SaddleTrace tell me which saddle to buy?

No, and it does not try to. SaddleTrace is a measuring instrument for people who already know how to fit a saddle. It records the shape of a back accurately and repeatably so you can compare an animal against itself over time, or against a tree in your hand. It is not a veterinary or diagnostic tool, and it is not a substitute for a qualified saddle fitter or a vet.

Reporting a problem

If something is wrong, email SUPPORT_EMAIL_HERE and include:

  • Your iPhone model and iOS version — Settings ▸ General ▸ About.
  • The SaddleTrace version number, shown in the app's Settings screen.
  • What you were doing, what you expected, and what happened instead.
  • A screenshot if the problem is something you can see.
  • If a particular scan is affected and you are willing to share it, the exported scan archive. It contains the photograph and measurements of the animal, so only send it if you are happy for it to be looked at.
